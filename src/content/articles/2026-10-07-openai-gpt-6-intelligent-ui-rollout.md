OpenAI said on October 7, 2026 that it is rolling out GPT-6 to ChatGPT users worldwide. The release comes with "Intelligent UI," a feature that lets the chatbot answer with charts, buttons, forms and small working apps instead of plain text alone. A month ago GPT-6 was only available to paying customers. This release brings a version of it to all tiers of a service that OpenAI says more than 1.2 billion people use every week.

## What happened

OpenAI says it trained GPT-6 to build each answer from a mix of text, images and interactive parts, and to pick the mix based on the question. In OpenAI's examples, a recipe comes with a cooking timeline next to it, a road trip shows up as stops on a map, and a statistics idea such as the central limit theorem or the Monty Hall problem becomes a diagram users can adjust. Users can also ask for a tool on the spot, such as a bill splitter, a retirement savings calculator or a simple retro-style game that runs inside the chat. OpenAI says the model is also trained to recognise when a plain text answer is the best choice.

The feature runs on two pieces. One is a library of ready-made interface components that are built into the app and can be streamed. The other is a compiler that builds the interface while the model is still writing, so parts of it appear on screen before the full response is done. OpenAI says it expanded its training methods to grade the interfaces the model makes for clarity, usefulness and completeness. It also admits the model's "design judgment" still needs work.

The rollout begins today for ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise subscribers in the Chat tab. Free and Go users get it starting tomorrow. For Enterprise customers, access depends on their workplace admin settings. Paid tiers get a model called GPT-6 Sol and Free and Go tiers get GPT-6 Luna. OpenAI describes both as tuned for everyday conversation, but the announcement does not explain how they differ. The models behind ChatGPT's Work and Codex features are not changing in this release.

## Faster answers and safety claims

The other main change is speed. Reasoning models think through a problem before they reply, and until now users had to wait for that thinking to finish before seeing anything. GPT-6 can start answering while it is still thinking, sending the answer in pieces. OpenAI says it trained the model to weigh how long the user has been waiting while keeping the finished answer coherent and accurate.

OpenAI's figures all come from its own internal testing:

- **Agentic tasks:** On everyday agentic tasks (multi-step jobs the model carries out for the user), GPT-6 Extra High reportedly starts answering as quickly as GPT-5.6 Medium while scoring higher overall than GPT-5.6 Extra High.
- **Web search:** On questions that need a web search, GPT-6 Instant reportedly starts answering 44% sooner on average than GPT-5.6 Instant.
- **Hard questions:** On a set of difficult problems, GPT-6 reportedly addressed the key part of the user's question more often than GPT-5.6.

On safety, OpenAI says GPT-6 builds on advances from Astra, an earlier GPT-6 model. Compared with GPT-5.6 Sol, the company says GPT-6 is better at resisting attempts to get around its safety training, particularly attacks that unfold over several turns of a conversation. It also says GPT-6 is clearer about what it can and can't do. Safety training was updated using lessons from real-world use, with a focus on cyberattacks, biological threats and violence. OpenAI also says the model is better at avoiding needless refusals of harmless requests. More detail is promised in a separate system card, which this article does not draw on.

## Why it matters and what's uncertain

Intelligent UI changes what a chatbot answer can be: instead of a block of text, a reply can be a small interactive app. OpenAI presents this as a step toward a bigger goal. In the company's words, "Instead of people adapting to software, software will adapt to people." If that works at scale, ChatGPT could take over some tasks people now use separate apps or websites for.

Several questions remain open:

- **Independent testing:** All the speed and quality figures come from OpenAI's internal evaluations, and outside benchmarks haven't been published yet.
- **Sol vs. Luna:** The announcement doesn't say how much less capable Luna is than Sol, so the gap between free and paid users is unclear.
- **Reliability:** OpenAI hasn't said how dependable the AI-built tools are. For example, it hasn't explained how users can check the numbers in a generated savings calculator.
- **Developer access:** The post covers only the consumer ChatGPT app. It doesn't say whether Intelligent UI will come to OpenAI's API.
