Anthropic, the company behind the Claude family of AI models, filed publicly for an initial public offering on Monday, October 26, 2026. It plans to trade under the ticker "ANT." The filing shows first-half revenue more than 13 times higher than a year earlier and a much smaller operating loss. It also shows a net loss of tens of billions of dollars and a governance setup that leaves control with the company's seven co-founders. The listing is likely to be one of the most closely watched U.S. stock debuts in years. It will also be an early public test of whether investors will fund a frontier AI developer whose structure limits their say over how it is run.

## What happened

Anthropic submitted its registration statement to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). This document, known as an S-1, is the disclosure a company must file before selling shares to the public. Drafts of the prospectus had been leaking for weeks beforehand, as TechCrunch and the Financial Times reported.

According to the filing:

- **Revenue:** $16.3 billion in the first six months of 2026, compared with $1.2 billion in the first half of 2025.
- **Operating loss:** $693 million, down from $2.1 billion a year earlier. Operating loss measures how much the core business lost before items like financing costs and accounting adjustments.
- **Net loss:** $80.7 billion. The company says this comes mostly from noncash accounting adjustments tied to its convertible debt, which is borrowing that can be turned into shares. A noncash charge reduces reported earnings, but no cash actually leaves the company.
- **Profitability warning:** In standard risk-factor language, Anthropic says it "may not achieve or sustain profitability in the future."

The prospectus also gives a detailed picture of the company's business customers. As of the end of September 2026:

- more than 11,900 customers were spending at least $100,000 a year
- about 1,900 were spending at least $1 million a year
- about 250 were spending at least $10 million a year

The company has not yet said how much money it wants to raise or what price range it will set for its shares. Those figures usually appear in a later amendment to the filing, shortly before the shares are marketed to investors.

## The control structure

The most debated part of the filing is about governance. All seven co-founders will hold their stakes through an entity called the "Founder LLC": Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah. Together, that entity will control 50.1% of shareholder voting power. In practice, that means a simple majority on any matter put to a shareholder vote.

Anthropic is registered as a public benefit corporation. That is a legal form that requires a company to weigh a stated public mission alongside profits. The company says the founder-control arrangement is meant to protect its AI safety mission once it has public shareholders. A second layer of oversight comes from the Long-Term Benefit Trust, a body Anthropic set up in 2023 to keep the company focused on that mission. Under the filing, the Trust will have the power to appoint most of Anthropic's board of directors. Separately, TechCrunch reported earlier this month that Anthropic is committing 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund.

## Why it matters

The arrangement means the founders' voting power will not match their economic ownership. Many tech companies have used dual-class or similar structures to give founders control, Meta and Alphabet among them. Critics say Anthropic's case raises sharper questions because of what it builds. In its own prospectus, the company warns that its technology could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity." Combining that warning with limited shareholder influence makes the debate harder to settle. On one view, insulating safety-focused founders from short-term market pressure is the right choice. On the other, it concentrates power over a potentially dangerous technology in very few hands.

The financial figures matter beyond Anthropic too. Revenue growth on this scale, alongside a narrowing operating loss, gives public markets one of the first audited looks at the economics of a leading AI model developer.

## What's uncertain

Several important details are still unknown:

- the size of the offering, the share price and the resulting valuation
- the timing of the listing and which stock exchange it will use
- the exact accounting mechanics behind the $80.7 billion net loss
- how the Founder LLC and the Long-Term Benefit Trust would settle a disagreement, since each holds a separate lever of control

IPO filings are often amended before shares begin trading, and the terms could change before Anthropic lists.
